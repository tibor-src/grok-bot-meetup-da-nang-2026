One bot, one job.

Tibor Sekelj

Until recently, Cursor.

Community developer, and before that technical support. I still work on human-agent collaboration. I run Grok Bot as a staff, not as one long chat.

The failure mode

One bot that does travel, mail, hardware, the books, and the reminders. It forgets which hat it is wearing. You spend the day correcting it.

Four parts

  • Name the job
  • Give each bot one job, including who may send
  • Put the check on a routine
  • Share a working bot as a template

The name is the job

First two letters of the purpose, turned into a person. If they do not make a clean name, do not force one.

A second job is a second bot

Troy does not pick up hardware because he was helpful on Tuesday. Helpful is how a specialist becomes a generalist.

Coordinator, then specialists

I used to keep a chief of staff, Costa, separate from the people who do the work. She coordinated. She did not book the trip, configure the hardware, or do the books.

Who owns the work

  • Troy owns travel
  • Hal owns hardware
  • Ace owns CodeMango and Xolo accounting

She did not redo their work, and she did not ping me if one of them had already told me.

One coordinator

Two people doing the same job is worse than one generalist. I am folding coordination into the primary bot and removing Costa. The specialists stay.

Only one bot sends mail

Max sends email. Everyone else drafts. Max sends once, after I have said yes. No second ask. Sia owns Signal. One inbox, one owner.

Why that rule exists

If every bot can send, you get three replies to the same person, in your name, and you cannot take them back.

Routines

A saved prompt on a clock or an event, owned by the bot whose job it is. I use a morning pass and a weekly pass. I am not reading you the prompts.

Silence is the default

If the check finds nothing, it stays quiet. A routine that messages you “nothing changed” every morning is how you learn to ignore the bot.

What you tell it

  • The scene, universe, or environment
  • Which characters get their own bots
  • Which side characters the narrator keeps
  • Then ask for a room: you and the narrator, or the cast

What is in the kit

Skills: getting started, character script, tune the style, set up the cast. Routines: a weekly play tune-up and a cast health check. Both stay quiet unless something needs a fix.
